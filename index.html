Case study — Mouse Factory (Course project)

Mouse Factory Project

In a course project I analyzed a large machining dataset to identify a statistically significant machining error affecting part quality. Using Excel and Minitab, I performed statistical analyses to detect variation and to propose a corrective action.

Methods & tools: Data cleaning and aggregation in Excel; hypothesis testing and control charts in Minitab.

Outcome: Identified the source of variation and proposed process adjustments to reduce the machining error. (Full artifacts are company/course-owned; summary and methodology are shown here.)

Reflection: This project illustrated the value of combining statistical methods with operational understanding — it felt like a real-world problem-solving experience and reinforced my interest in using data to drive operational improvements.

KSAs demonstrated: Statistical analysis, data cleaning, interpretation of results, and translating findings into practical process recommendations.

Reflection

How I am developing as an engineer

From solving problems to defining the right problem

Earlier in my engineering education, I tended to think of a good solution as the endpoint. My experiences outside the classroom have changed that perspective. At WSP, I saw how important verification and communication are. At Amazon, I saw how a solution has to work within a living operation and gain the support of people who use it. Through statistics, I have learned to be more careful about uncertainty and variation rather than relying only on intuition.

Going forward, I want to become better at working with ambiguity: defining problems clearly, deciding what information is actually needed, testing potential solutions, and communicating recommendations to people with different technical backgrounds. I also want to keep expanding my programming and automation skills beyond what is required for my degree.

Journal

Personal statement — freshman reflection

Since coming to college in August of 2023, my life and perspectives have changed dramatically. I am not the scared, wide-eyed freshman I was those nine months ago. I’ve learned about my relational goals, my career goals, and my goals for who I want to be.

At the start of the year, I was scared to make friends, and planned on relying on the honors program to do so. However, the actual course work and assignments in Honors challenged me to step out of my comfort zone, leading my to join a FLO on campus, as well as reach out to people in my classes. Stepping out of my comfort zone in that way has helped me develop a rich and diverse network of friends that I can’t wait to carry into and expand on over the next three years.

I also learned a lot about what I want to do with my life, from a career standpoint. I went from intending to be a chemical engineer Freshman year, to being passionate about pursuing an industrial systems degree, which I am about to ETAM and apply for. Working and talking with a variety of engineers has showed me I want to be in a career that is people-focused, while maintain a high technical complexity and intellectual stimulation, and I think I’ve found that in systems engineering. Honors has also shaped how I plan to achieve this goal- I’ve found concrete steps to take such as applying to join a research lab next year, talking to mentors and professors in my field, and learning more through my own independent research.

Going into Honors, I thought that my only priority in college would be academic excellence. However, this year I’ve learned that for me, life to the fullest means blending relationships, hard work, studying, and health altogether, without holding any one as my number one priority. I’ve been surprised that college, at the end of the day, has taught me less about the importance of grades, and more about the importance of learning- about other people as well as yourself. My goals for college are no longer ‘have the highest possible GPA’ but ‘become the best possible person you can be’, which is a framework I hope to live the next three years by.

In the future, I hope to align my college plans with the goal of becoming the best possible person. For me, this means balancing several good things, instead of prioritizing one thing as the best. It means keeping up with my family through long phone calls, as well as studying long hours for my midterm exams. It means going to the gym to maintain my health, and also going to get ice cream with my friends. It means talking to professors even when its scary, applying to things I don’t feel qualified for so that I can learn them, and stepping up to tasks that I am not an expert in, because that is where growth happens.

For me, a successful time in college means that I will have truly learned the fundamental concepts of engineering, as well as gained the interpersonal skills need to apply those concepts through work. Being true to myself means pushing myself to be the best I can be, and at the end of the day, that is what I believe life is about. I think that engineering is the right and best way for me to do that, because it is a job where I will be able to work hard to create good things, that will safely serve and benefit the greater population. Besides, so much growth happens in society from collaboration, and since that is one of my favorite parts of engineering, I know that my love of interacting with people will be a benefit to the field.

There are still many things I hope to do in college. I want to continue to develop leadership skills through time in organizations, as well as spend time serving my community and peers, because I know a life well lived is a life spent serving others. I also want to save myself plenty of time for my studies, so I can become the best possible engineer I can be. Finally, I hope to use my capstone as a way to develop expertise in the area of systems engineering, and hopefully do so in a way that can clarify or fix a system for the good of the world, or even just the people here at A&M.

College is a learning experience, and it’s already taught me so much. I’ve learned how much I value relationships, the importance of hard work, and of balancing ones time. Over the next four years, I can’t wait to not only develop technical skills as an engineer, but to develop as a person as well so that I can ‘engineer’ a difference for good in the world.
